# About page photography

Sourced from Unsplash for this page specifically (`lessons.md` §41: new
pictures come from Unsplash, never from frames another section already
carries), downloaded and converted locally to WebP.

Every photograph here has a reason to be in the story. None of them is on the
page because it is a black-and-white fashion picture:

| Local file | What it is, and why it is in the page | Photographer | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `lineup.webp` | Four models against a plain wall wearing numbered casting tags. The industry's judgement ritual in one frame, and the only numbers the page uses are the ones on those tags. It carries the opening and chapter I. | Sou Jest | https://unsplash.com/photos/XPfhH2ln0og |
| `sitting.webp` | A plain wall, a softbox in frame, one person. This is what the money in the bill actually buys, so it is the object chapter III buries. | Allan Lainez | https://unsplash.com/photos/2SKQF1MuakI |
| `waiting.webp` | Models on a low wall between calls. It carries the chapter about silence without the page having to say anything sentimental about it. | Sou Jest | https://unsplash.com/photos/J8mvdksqk9Y |
| `book.webp` | A stack of prints in someone's hands: the object the whole company exists to get right. The colophon's one plate. | Luiz Rogério Nunes | https://unsplash.com/photos/wtLSUniUJgs |

Grayscale and a small grade are baked into the files rather than applied as a
CSS filter. These plates are never shown in colour, and three of them are
scaled and moved on a pinned scroll stage, where a live `filter: grayscale()`
on a full-bleed image is paid for on every frame. The register is the site's
(`docs/design-language/03-banned-ui.md` §7.3); only the place it is applied
moved.

All four source pages described the photographs as free to use under the
Unsplash License when they were downloaded, September 26 2026. None is an
Unsplash+ asset; that was checked per photograph (`premium` and `plus` both
false) before downloading, because the first portrait chosen for the opening
was Unsplash+ and could not be used.

The three portraits in The Collective are not from here: they are the real
people, and they live in `public/assets/`.

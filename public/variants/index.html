From the editor. Before an agency meets you, they meet what you sent. This issue is about making that first look the right one: the digitals they ask for, a card worth pulling, and an honest answer when nobody writes back.

Before the runway, there is the submission.

The card

Nine ways to wear your name.

A comp card has two sides and one job: to be pulled from a pile and kept. Pholio composes yours from your own photographs. It finds where your face falls in each frame and sets your name in the space that’s left, so no two cards come out quite alike, even in the same edition.

“Where your face falls decides where your name goes.”

Front and back, five and a half by eight and a half inches, print-ready or sent as a file. The Standard is free. The other eight editions come with Studio+.

Intelligence

In conversation with the machine.

What do you look at?
The photograph. Shot type, focus, light, background. Whether it’s the close-up an agency asked for. Never the face itself.
Will you write for me?
A bio, a note to an agency, a message you’re unsure of. Only from what you’ve told me. I don’t invent a credit or a measurement.
What about my old card?
Send it. I’ll read the words printed on it, name, measurements, agency, and fill in your profile. The words, not the pictures.
And when nobody replies?
I’ll count what really happened: opens, card pulls, dates. Then I’ll tell you plainly when a submission is past its usual read.

“I read the photograph. Never the face.”
